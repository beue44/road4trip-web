Privacy policy

Road4Trip

The short version: Road4Trip has no server of its own. The app sends nothing to the developer, and the developer collects nothing. On its own, the app only sends data to Apple (your iCloud, maps, routes, weather) and to swisstopo (elevation, coordinates only). Data only goes to others when you trigger it yourself – for example when you share a trip, turn on your location during a group trip or open a route in Google Maps.

What data is collected

The developer of Road4Trip collects no data about you. Road4Trip has no server of its own, no user account, no registration, no analytics tools, no advertising and no trackers.

What the app stores is kept on your device and in your own iCloud; events you add go into your calendar. Contributions to a trip that someone else has shared are stored in that person’s iCloud. The developer has no access to any of this.

For some features, data goes to services outside the device: to Apple for maps, routes, place search and weather, and to swisstopo for elevation gain.

Road4Trip only gives data to others when you trigger it yourself – for example when you share a trip, turn on your location during a group trip or open a route in Google Maps. The sections below explain what is transmitted in each case.

Your routes and trips

The routes, stops, dates, notes and ratings you enter are stored on your iPhone.

If you are signed in to iCloud, Road4Trip additionally syncs them between your own devices – iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch. Apple’s privacy terms for iCloud apply.

Photos are not transferred by default. Whether they are included is up to you, under Settings → Sync.

Without signing in to iCloud everything stays on the device, and the app keeps working in full.

When you share a trip

If you share a trip via “Plan with others”, Road4Trip stores a copy of that one trip in your iCloud and grants access to the people you invite.

The name, notes, date, means of transport, distance and travel time are transferred, along with the stops including their address and coordinates – no photos, no rating, no history and no other routes.

Those invited can suggest stops and meeting points and vote on them. These contributions are also stored in your iCloud and are visible to everyone involved in the trip.

The app looks for a meeting point halfway between the home of the person suggesting it and the start. To do so, it asks Apple for a café, car park or restaurant near this midpoint. Only the place found is transferred, not the home. But because the place lies roughly in the middle, it gives a rough idea of the direction and distance the person suggesting it is travelling from.

Sharing only happens when you trigger it. If you delete the route or reset the app, the shared copy and the contributions remain. The developer of Road4Trip is not part of this share and cannot see it.

Backup in iCloud Drive

If you turn backups on, Road4Trip stores a copy of your routes in your personal iCloud Drive – in the “Road4Trip” folder. A switch decides whether photos are included.

This data stays in your own Apple Account. It does not go to the developer of Road4Trip and not to third parties; Apple’s privacy terms for iCloud apply.

You can delete stored backups yourself at any time in the Files app.

Calendar

If you add a trip as an event, Road4Trip asks for permission to write events – not to read them. So the app does not see your calendar.

The event contains the name of the trip, its start and duration, the address of the first stop, the names of the stops, the kilometres and your notes on the trip, as well as a reminder one hour before. It goes into your default calendar. If that calendar belongs to another account, such as Google or Exchange, iOS syncs the event there.

Location

Road4Trip needs your location only for group trips. The app only asks for permission when you turn on “Share my location” there, and then shares your location with the people involved in the trip – more under “Group trip (location)”.

Otherwise the app does not determine your location and does not show it on any map. If you start a route from your current location, the maps app determines it itself.

You can withdraw the permission in the iPhone settings at any time – the app remains usable.

Place search and route calculation

Whenever you search for a place, or the app shows a map or calculates a route, these requests go through Apple’s maps service. Depending on the case, Apple receives the search text, the map area shown, for “Suggestions along the route” the area around the stops together with the chosen type of place, or for routes the coordinates of the stops together with the means of transport.

Road4Trip also calculates routes on its own, for example when you open a route. Apple’s privacy terms apply.

The developer of Road4Trip learns nothing about them.

Elevation gain (swisstopo)

When Road4Trip calculates the ascent and descent of a route, the app sends the coordinates of the calculated route to the map service of the Federal Office of Topography (swisstopo). An elevation profile comes back, of which the app only uses the elevation values.

Only coordinates are transmitted – no name and no identifier. As with any request on the internet, swisstopo sees the IP address, and iOS also sends technical details such as the app and system version.

This happens automatically after every route calculation, but only for the means of transport Hiking, On foot and Bicycle and only for trips within an area around Switzerland that also includes border areas of neighbouring countries.

The privacy policy of swisstopo applies.

Group trip (location)

In a trip that has been shared with you, you can share your location with the other people involved while you are on the road. This is off until you explicitly turn on “Share my location”.

Your location then goes to the people involved in this particular trip – to nobody else. Sharing continues when you leave the screen or the app goes into the background – until you turn it off. For this, the app also determines your location in the background; iOS shows this in the status bar, Road4Trip in the “Group trip running” bar.

Only your most recent location is stored, together with the time, the device name (such as “iPhone”) and a pseudonymous iCloud identifier. It is overwritten with every update – no track and no movement profile is created. It is sent at most once a minute and only after you have moved at least 250 metres.

The first person to turn on “Share my location” also creates an entry for the group trip – with the start time and their pseudonymous iCloud identifier. It is valid for twelve hours.

When you turn off “Share my location” or tap “Stop” in the “Group trip running” bar, your location is deleted immediately. The same happens if you revoke the app’s location permission or the group trip expires after twelve hours.

If the app is closed while you are sharing – for example by a crash – it withdraws your location the next time it starts. Until then, it is no longer shown after 30 minutes without an update and is deleted as soon as someone in the group opens “Group trip”.

The app deletes the entry for the group trip once the twelve hours have passed, as soon as someone in the group opens “Group trip”; if nobody opens it again, it remains. If the trip is no longer shared, Road4Trip does not delete any of it.

The data is stored in the iCloud of the person who shared the trip. The developer of Road4Trip has no access to it.

Weather (Apple WeatherKit)

If a trip has a date within the next ten days, Road4Trip fetches the weather for it from Apple – automatically, as soon as the trip is displayed, including in the list. To do so, the coordinates of individual stops and the planned time are sent to Apple’s weather service.

Only this information is transmitted – no name and no identifier. As with any request on the internet, Apple sees the IP address. Your own location is not used for this, and the forecast is not stored permanently.

Apple’s privacy terms apply.

Navigation in Apple Maps and Google Maps

When you start a route, Road4Trip hands the coordinates of your stops to the maps app you chose. From that moment on, its privacy terms apply.

This handover only happens when you trigger it yourself.

Your rights

The app sends no data to the developer. The developer therefore holds no data from the app that could be disclosed or deleted on request.

If you e-mail the developer, they have your message, your address and the app version from the subject line. You can ask them to give you information about this data or to delete it.

Your content is stored on your device and in your Apple Account. What is in iCloud can be deleted in the iPhone settings under your name → “iCloud”, and backups also in the Files app.

Contributions to a trip that someone else has shared – suggestions, votes, your location during a group trip and the entry for a group trip you were the first to turn on – are stored in that person’s iCloud.

You can withdraw votes, and you delete your location with the “Share my location” switch or with “Stop” in the “Group trip running” bar. You cannot delete suggestions or the entry for the group trip in the app.

Changes

If features are added that let data leave the device, this statement is updated in the same version, and “What’s new” mentions the change.
